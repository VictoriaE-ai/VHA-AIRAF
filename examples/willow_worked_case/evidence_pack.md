# Willow fictional evidence pack

Version 0.2.0 educational example | 22 October 2026

**Every organisation, person, document, interview, exercise and observation below is invented.** These are authored scenario excerpts, not records of work conducted in a care provider. Evidence IDs let readers trace the illustrative judgements and disagree with them. No independent assessor review has occurred. This example is not validation, clinical advice, legal assurance or permission to deploy.

Willow has six staff in the relevant role/shift scope and proposes a local mock summariser using 40 wholly invented handovers. No real residents or care records are involved; no vendor endpoint or live record integration exists. Scope excludes diagnosis, treatment, resident-facing outputs and live operational use. A real product or changed scope needs fresh review.

The fictional decision matrix gives the manager stop authority, but Q21's scenario shows staff lack a usable intervention workflow and allocated review time. Both facts are retained: an organisational role assignment does not prove effective oversight. The shared-folder weakness likewise remains despite positive infrastructure and training findings.

Evidence mode records describe the authored scenario's evidence basis, not real events. “Mixed” can include documents, fictional interviews and partly completed checks. Scores below are illustrative judgements, not machine-derived interpretations of prose. Dates and action owners are fictional; no reminder or commitment is created.

## E01

**Q1 — Strategy linked to care objectives**

Scenario excerpt: An approved one-page digital plan links documentation quality to protected staff time. The manager owns monthly review, a four-hour preparation allocation and a decision log. A recorded decision deferred tool purchase until a use-case brief was complete.

Illustrative score: **3/5**. Rationale: All essential plan components are in operation; repeated monitoring and corrective review are not evidenced.

Next action: Review delivery against the plan and record exceptions.

## E02

**Q2 — Leadership understanding of AI**

Scenario excerpt: The manager and deputy completed a recorded scenario: a generated summary omitted a medication change. Both identified uncertainty, retained professional responsibility, rejected the draft and named the quality lead for escalation. Contacts were checked during the exercise.

Illustrative score: **3/5**. Rationale: Role-relevant scenario covers benefits, errors, data/bias risks, accountability and escalation; no longitudinal learning evidence.

Next action: Repeat checks after material changes.

## E03

**Q3 — Defined use cases and measurable value**

Scenario excerpt: A reviewed brief defines administrative handover-draft preparation for staff, excludes care recommendations and resident-facing outputs, names the deputy as owner and compares a revised manual template. It defines time, omission and correction measures, a two-week baseline-collection plan and criteria to abandon the idea. Staff reviewed feasibility.

Illustrative score: **3/5**. Rationale: Complete reviewed brief supports preparation; benefits remain prospective.

Next action: Collect baseline before any pilot comparison.

## E04

**Q4 — Data fitness for the intended purpose**

Scenario excerpt: A synthetic record set contains 40 invented handovers. A manual check found six incomplete timestamps and three ambiguous names. A draft quality checklist covers completeness, consistency, accuracy against the scenario specification and timeliness. No acceptance thresholds or completed correction review exist.

Illustrative score: **2/5**. Rationale: Quality assessment is partly applied, but purpose-specific acceptance criteria and unresolved defects limit the score.

Next action: Define justified criteria, correct defects and recheck.

## E05

**Q5 — Data-quality processes**

Scenario excerpt: A draft correction procedure assigns a records owner, validation and escalation steps, a change log and downstream notification. Staff tested three corrections, but two lacked reviewer initials and no repeat-defect review occurred.

Illustrative score: **2/5**. Rationale: Traceable quality process is partly implemented; incomplete correction records prevent score 3.

Next action: Complete correction traceability and verify the process.

## E06

## E10

## E21

**Q21 — Effective human oversight**

Scenario excerpt: The proposal says a senior worker will sign off summaries. During the scenario the worker could flag an error but could not edit or reject the locked output, had no allocated review time and was unsure who could suspend the tool. This contradicted the draft claim of full human oversight.

Illustrative score: **1/5**. Rationale: Nominal sign-off with unreliable authority, capacity and intervention meets score 1, not effective oversight.

Next action: Provide workable rejection/stop authority, review time and tested fallback.
